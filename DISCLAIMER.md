## 2. Purpose of the Project

The Project exists to collect, organize, preserve, analyze, contextualize, and explain publicly available information concerning cybersecurity incidents, vulnerabilities, breaches, exploitation, security disclosures, threat activity, and related technical matters.

Our objective is straightforward:

**Document what is publicly known, distinguish fact from allegation, explain the technology, preserve the supporting evidence, and correct the record when better information becomes available.**

The Project may provide substantially more technical detail, correlation, historical context, or analysis than the original sources from which underlying facts were obtained.

Such analysis represents independent research based on information reasonably available at the time of publication.

## 4. Fact, Attribution, and Technical Analysis

The Project endeavors to distinguish among:

**Confirmed Fact** — Information supported by sufficiently authoritative or reliable public evidence.

**Attributed Statement** — Information reported, alleged, claimed, or stated by an identified third party.

**Technical Analysis** — Conclusions, explanations, correlations, assessments, or interpretations developed from available technical and factual information.

**Developing Information** — Information concerning an event that remains incomplete, evolving, disputed, or subject to further confirmation.

**Unknown** — Information for which available evidence does not support a responsible conclusion.

A statement made by a threat actor, company, researcher, journalist, vendor, government agency, or other third party is not automatically adopted by InfinityGrid as independently established fact merely because that statement is documented by the Project.

Where material uncertainty exists, the Project intends to communicate that uncertainty.

## 5. No Accusation Beyond the Evidence

Reference to an organization in connection with a cybersecurity incident does not necessarily mean that the organization acted negligently, unlawfully, recklessly, incompetently, or improperly.

Cybersecurity incidents can occur despite substantial security controls.

Similarly, the existence of a vulnerability within a product does not independently establish that the vulnerability caused a particular breach.

The existence of a CVE does not establish exploitation.

The existence of exploitation does not establish compromise of every vulnerable system.

A threat actor's claim does not establish that the claim is true.

A company's denial does not independently establish that an allegation is false.

The Project evaluates the available evidence and attempts to describe only what that evidence reasonably supports.

---

## 6. Technical Correlation Does Not Equal Proven Causation

CVE-to-Breach and other Project materials may identify relationships among vulnerabilities, products, attack techniques, threat actors, timelines, infrastructure, campaigns, and security incidents.

Unless explicitly supported by authoritative evidence, such relationships should not be interpreted as definitive proof of causation.

For example, identifying that:

* a vulnerable product was reportedly present,
* exploitation was technically possible,
* a vulnerability was actively exploited during the relevant period, and
* an incident occurred during that period

does **not**, by itself, prove that the vulnerability caused the incident.

Where causation has not been established, the Project should characterize the relationship as a correlation, possibility, reported connection, or other appropriately qualified assessment.

---

## 7. Developing Incidents

BreachWatch may document incidents before every material fact is known.

Cybersecurity investigations frequently evolve over days, weeks, months, or years. Initial statements may subsequently be corrected, expanded, contradicted, or superseded.

Accordingly, developing incident records should not be interpreted as final determinations.

Readers should consult the latest record, cited sources, correction history, and subsequent disclosures before relying upon an early incident assessment.

---

## 8. Historical Record and Updates

The Project may preserve earlier statements, timelines, disclosures, claims, or assessments even after subsequent information changes the understanding of an incident.

Preservation does not mean that outdated information continues to represent the Project's current assessment.

Maintaining historical information can be essential to understanding:

* what was publicly known at a particular time;
* how an organization's disclosures changed;
* how technical understanding evolved;
* when affected individuals were notified;
* when vulnerabilities became publicly known;
* and how an incident progressed.

Material corrections should be documented rather than silently rewriting history whenever practical.

## 9. Corrections and Right of Response

Accuracy matters more than defending an earlier conclusion.

Organizations, researchers, vendors, affected parties, and members of the public are encouraged to identify factual errors and provide authoritative evidence supporting a correction.

A correction request should identify:

1. The specific statement believed to be inaccurate;
2. Why the statement is inaccurate;
3. The proposed correction; and
4. Supporting primary or otherwise authoritative evidence.

Substantiated factual errors should be corrected.

Material corrections may be documented in the repository's correction history.

A request to remove accurate, lawfully published, public-interest information merely because that information is unfavorable, embarrassing, critical, or commercially inconvenient will not, by itself, establish that the information is inaccurate.

Likewise, criticism, disagreement, or the existence of an alternative interpretation does not automatically establish factual error.

InfinityGrid reserves editorial discretion regarding the presentation, organization, contextualization, and analysis of Project material, subject to its commitment to factual accuracy and responsible correction.
