# InfinityGrid Trust Infrastructure Review Methodology

## Purpose

The InfinityGrid Trust Infrastructure methodology evaluates cybersecurity services and public-private cyber capabilities that have become sufficiently important to influence security decisions beyond their original organizational boundaries.

The methodology is designed to answer:

- What does the system do?
- Who depends upon it?
- What information enters it?
- What authority does it exercise?
- How is that authority governed?
- What controls are publicly established?
- What remains unknown?
- What happens if the system fails or changes ownership?

## Research Standard

InfinityGrid follows one rule above all others:

# Evidence Before Narrative

We do not begin with a conclusion and search for evidence supporting it.

The public evidence determines the conclusion.

## Evidence Categories

### E4 — Established

Supported by authoritative evidence such as:

- government records
- court records
- regulator findings
- official company disclosures
- direct technical documentation
- published contractual or policy material

### E3 — Corroborated

Supported by multiple credible independent sources or strong technical evidence.

### E2 — Reported

Credibly reported but not independently established.

### E1 — Claimed

A claim exists but is not independently verified.

### E0 — Unknown

The public record is insufficient to establish the answer.

## Not Publicly Established

This term has a specific meaning inside InfinityGrid research.

**NOT PUBLICLY ESTABLISHED** does not mean:

- the control does not exist
- the organization failed
- wrongdoing occurred
- no private agreement exists

It means InfinityGrid has not located sufficient public evidence to mark the matter established.

## Review Domains

Every Trust Infrastructure review may examine the following areas.

### 1. Ownership

- legal entity
- jurisdiction
- operator
- governance structure
- key-person dependency

### 2. Security

- authentication
- encryption
- network protection
- application security
- vulnerability management
- incident response
- security testing

### 3. Governance

- policies
- external oversight
- board responsibility
- government relationships
- formal agreements
- audit rights

### 4. Data Provenance

- source of information
- method of ingestion
- verification
- corroboration
- attribution
- correction

### 5. Data Retention

- source-data retention
- archive retention
- deletion
- provider withdrawal
- access control
- archival access logging

### 6. Government Integration

- government contributions
- government recommendations
- remediation partnerships
- investigative-data transfer
- notification relationships

### 7. Auditability

- independent security audits
- government audits
- technical certification
- control validation
- published assessment results

### 8. Concentration

- public dependency
- enterprise dependency
- security-product dependency
- government dependency
- replaceability

### 9. Continuity

- outage planning
- service closure
- succession
- disaster recovery
- data portability
- replacement capability

### 10. Change of Control

- acquisition
- merger
- sale
- insolvency
- restructuring
- jurisdictional change
